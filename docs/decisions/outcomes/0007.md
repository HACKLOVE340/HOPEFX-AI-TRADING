# Outcome 0007 — Ship everything except the hand-landmark model

- Decision: 0007
- Status: observed
- Observed: 2026-09-13

## Expected

Installing a detector becomes a configuration change rather than a code change.
Two capability rows stay staged "on something that reads as unverifiable".

## Actual outcome

**Reversed two days later.** 0007 was accepted on 2026-09-07; 0009 superseded it
on 2026-09-09 and shipped the model, fetched at deploy time by
`scripts/fetch_hand_model.py`.

The premise turned out to be false rather than the reasoning wrong. 0007 assumed
the camera path was absent and that the two `vision.*` rows could not be made
live without vendoring a binary. 0009's title says what was found —
*"the camera was there all along"* — and once absence was modelled as a **state**
(`landmarkStatus: unconfigured`, with pointer gestures still working) rather than
as a break, the deploy step became safe to skip and the rows became live.

## Lessons

This is the repository's clearest case of a record paying for itself, and it paid
in 48 hours. The reversal was cheap *because* the reasoning was written down: the
premise was inspectable, so it could be checked and found wrong. A decision made
the same way with no record would have been re-argued from scratch, or more
likely never revisited, and the two rows would still be staged.

The durable lesson is the modelling move, not the vision feature: **absence
modelled as a state is skippable; absence modelled as a break is a
prerequisite.** That pattern generalises to every optional artefact this platform
fetches, and it is the reason 0009's deploy step costs an operator who never
enables camera gestures exactly nothing.

The superseding record is 0009; its own outcome is recorded separately.
