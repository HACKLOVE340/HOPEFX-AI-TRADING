# Outcome 0012 — The change-record gate warns rather than blocks

- Decision: 0012
- Status: observed
- Observed: 2026-09-13

## Expected

Adoption becomes voluntary and therefore visible. The record stated its own
starting number — *"the first `--report` reads **20% (1 of 5)**, which is a real
starting number rather than a target met by force"* — and set the condition for
revisiting: *"If coverage does not rise, the warning is being scrolled past and
the honest response is to enforce, not to lower the target."*

## Actual outcome

**It rose.** Measured 2026-09-13:

| Range | Changes | Needing a prediction | With one | Coverage |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Since the decision (2026-09-10 → HEAD) | 104 | 48 | 17 | **35%** |
| The whole branch (`origin/main..HEAD`) | 582 | 261 | 28 | 11% |

20% → 35% over the four days since the record. The whole-branch figure of 11% is
mostly pre-decision history and is the baseline the 35% is moving away from, not
a competing measurement.

So the objection the record was answering — that a warning is decorative — is not
borne out yet, and neither is the target. 35% is a long way from Chapter 6's
100% of core-tier.

## Lessons

The decision's real contribution was insisting the KPI be measurable before the
policy was chosen. *"A warning whose effect is never measured is exactly what the
objection is about"* — and because `--report` exists, this entry is arithmetic
rather than an impression. Every soft gate should ship with the query that would
prove it soft.

The condition for escalating is met or unmet by a number, so nobody has to
re-argue it. Not met: coverage rose. The honest next review is whether 35%
plateaus, which needs more commits rather than more discussion — hence the second
half of this entry is a `pending` obligation on the *escalation* question, tracked
here rather than by reopening the decision.

`CHANGE_RECORD_ENFORCE=1` still flips the policy with no code change, which keeps
the escalation one environment variable away.
