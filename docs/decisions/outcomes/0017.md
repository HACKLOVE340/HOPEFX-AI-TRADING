# Outcome 0017 — An incomplete coverage baseline may be extended, under four conditions

- Decision: 0017
- Status: observed
- Observed: 2026-09-13

## Expected

A baseline that is honest about being incomplete, extendable only under four
stated conditions, so that recording debt stays cheaper than hiding it — and so
that the list moves in one direction.

## Actual outcome

It shrank, which is the direction that matters.
`docs/COVERAGE_UNMEASURABLE.txt` held **249** entries at the decision
(2026-09-10, commit `409883b5`) and holds **228** on 2026-09-13 — 21 modules
removed by being measured rather than by being excused.

`scripts/pre_commit_coverage.py` enforces the one-way property directly: a module
in the list is `DEBT` and "the list may only shrink", and raising a module's
coverage requires deleting its line rather than editing a number. The gate's
message says so at the point of failure — *"Not permission — raise it and delete
the line."*

The file's own header records why the entries exist and how the original reading
of them was itself a casualty of a broken measurement (`--cov=<dotted.module>`
re-importing numpy's C extension mid-process). That history survived in the
artefact, which is the cheapest place for it.

## Lessons

A debt list with a ratchet is a plan; a debt list without one is a list of
excuses, and the two look identical on the day they are written. The four
conditions are what made the difference, and the measurable consequence — 249 →
228 in three days — is the evidence they were not merely aspirational.

The second lesson is the one embedded in the header: when a measurement is
broken, everything it produced is suspect, including the *interpretation* of its
output. The entries meant something different from what everyone believed they
meant. Re-reading a baseline after fixing the tool that built it is not optional.
